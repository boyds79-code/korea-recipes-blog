Hotteok is Korea's answer to the perfect winter street snack—crispy on the outside, pillowy-soft inside, with a molten brown sugar filling that tastes like liquid cinnamon candy. The magic is in the yeasted dough, which gives these pancakes their characteristic chew, and in sealing the filling so tightly that the sugar syrup doesn't leak out while you're cooking. Once you nail the sealing technique, you'll be making these on repeat.

![Ingredients for hotteok](/images/blog/hotteok-sweet-filled-pancakes-with-brown-sugar-syrup/ingredients.jpg)

## Make the Dough

In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, granulated sugar, instant yeast, and salt. Pour in the warm milk and oil, then stir with a wooden spoon or your hands until a sticky, shaggy dough forms. **Knead for 3-4 minutes** right in the bowl—the dough will remain quite sticky, much stickier than bread dough, and that's exactly right. Lightly oil the surface of the dough, cover the bowl with plastic wrap or a damp towel, and let it rise in a warm spot for **1 hour until doubled in size**. If your kitchen is cold, set the bowl near (not on) a warm stovetop or in a turned-off oven with the light on.

## Prepare the Filling

While the dough rises, mix the brown sugar, cinnamon, chopped nuts (if using), and 1 tablespoon of flour in a small bowl. The flour is the secret—**it absorbs some of the moisture** from the melting sugar and helps the filling stay cohesive instead of turning into a thin syrup that bursts out. Set this aside.

## Shape and Fill the Pancakes

Once the dough has doubled, oil your hands generously—this dough is sticky by nature. Divide it into 6-8 equal portions (each about the size of a golf ball). Take one piece and flatten it into a disc in your palm, about 3-4 inches wide. Spoon **1-1½ tablespoons of filling** into the center. Now comes the critical sealing step: gather the edges of the dough up and over the filling like a little pouch, pinching and twisting the seams together firmly at the top. **Pinch multiple times and twist to create a tight seal**—this is 설탕 시럽이 새지 않게 봉하는 요령, the key to keeping the syrup from leaking. Turn the ball seam-side down and gently press it between your palms to flatten slightly into a disc. Set aside and repeat with the remaining dough.

> 💡 **Tip:** If your dough tears while sealing, it's too dry—re-oil your hands. If the filling leaks during cooking, you didn't pinch the seam tightly enough. Practice makes perfect!

## Cook the Hotteok

Heat a wide skillet or griddle over **medium to medium-low heat** and add about 1 tablespoon of oil. Place 2-3 filled dough discs seam-side down in the pan, leaving space between them. Let them cook untouched for **1-2 minutes until the bottoms start to set**. Then, using a flat spatula (a metal one works best), gently but firmly press down on each pancake to flatten it to about ½-inch thick. **Cook for 2-3 minutes until the bottom is deep golden brown**, then flip and press again lightly. Cook the second side for another **2-3 minutes until golden and crispy**. The filling inside should be melted and syrupy. Remove to a plate and repeat with the remaining pancakes, adding a bit more oil to the pan as needed.

## Serving and Substitutions

Hotteok is best eaten warm, straight from the pan, when the filling is still gooey. Let them cool for just **1-2 minutes** before biting in—the sugar syrup inside is molten hot and will burn your tongue if you're impatient. Serve them as a sweet breakfast, dessert, or midday snack with hot tea or coffee.

**Can't find instant yeast?** Use active dry yeast, but bloom it in the warm milk for 5 minutes first before mixing with the dry ingredients. **No brown sugar?** White granulated sugar works, though you'll lose some of that deep molasses flavor. **Nut-free?** Skip the nuts entirely or swap in sesame seeds or sunflower seeds for crunch. If you want a richer dough, substitute half the milk with warm water and add 1 tablespoon of melted butter. Some cooks add a pinch of sweet rice flour (glutinous rice flour) to the dough for extra chew, but all-purpose flour alone works beautifully.

Store any leftovers (rare, but it happens) in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days and reheat in a dry skillet over low heat to re-crisp the outside.
